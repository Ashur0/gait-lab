Gait Lab

Sixty creatures, each steered by a 154-weight neural network, on ground that will not stay flat. None of them are told what walking is. Every generation the twelve that travelled furthest are copied and mutated; the rest are discarded. Watch the ghosts converge.

No gradients

There is no backpropagation and no training data here. Selection and gaussian mutation alone. At 154 weights the search space is small enough that copying what worked beats computing what should work.

It learned to fly first

Verlet integration infers velocity from position, so every constraint correction silently became momentum. Evolution found that within ten generations and swam through the air, scoring ten times a real gait. Fixed by conserving centre-of-mass velocity whenever no foot is loaded.

Then it learned to cheat

With honest physics it coiled its legs, catapulted once and glided — ninety percent airborne. Distance alone is the wrong reward. Fitness is now distance × ground contact × posture, with hard limits on launch height and spine roll.

The ground is not flat

Two of the ten senses are a terrain preview: the slope just ahead and the slope just crossed. On flat ground a creature can memorise a rhythm; on hills it has to set the leg up before the slope arrives. Amplitude ramps in over the first 500px so a gait can establish itself before the terrain starts asking questions of it.

Reading the display

The solid figure is the current leader; the pale cloud behind it is the other fifty-nine, each drawn in its own frame so the whole population superimposes. A red ring marks a loaded foot. Early generations are chaos; the cloud tightening into one silhouette is the population converging on a gait.
